% === PARAGRAPH 1: The scenario ===

A United Nations Security Council (UNSC) meeting is underway to vote on a ceasefire agreement for a conflict that has been going on for years. Both sides are accusing the other party of war crimes without concrete. Online, a video surfaces showing soldiers from one party carrying out organised killings of unarmed civilians. The video quickly goes viral, and is shared by news outlets across social media. Diplomats condemn the killings and the UNSC imposes economic sanctions. The accused nation denies the allegations and claims that the video is fabricated. However, the damage is already done. Diplomatic relations have been lost and strict economic sanctions are in place. The latter eventually leads to the accused nation's economy collapsing and a forced withdrawal from the conflict.



% === PARAGRAPH 2: The aim of the essay ===

However, there is more to the story. The killings never happened. The video was a deepfake, generated by the government of the opposing nation to discredit the accused nation.
Generative artificial intelligence (Gen AI) has made significant advancements, with recently footage generated by Gen AI being almost indistinguishable from reality.
Until not very long ago, deepfakes were not very convincing, and were mostly used for entertainment purposes. But in 2026, the advancements of generative artificial intelligence could very well be used to fabricate a video with deepfake technology to influence international diplomacy.
This essay will explore the ethical implications of deepfakes in time of conflict or war.
